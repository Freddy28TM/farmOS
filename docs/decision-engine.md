# FarmOS Decision Engine

## 1. Purpose

The FarmOS Decision Engine is the core agricultural reasoning component of the system.

Its purpose is to transform validated environmental conditions and farm context into an explainable water-risk assessment.

The engine produces:

* Risk score
* Risk level
* Confidence
* Contributing factors
* Recommendation
* Explanation

The current implementation uses deterministic rules rather than an opaque AI model.

This makes the system predictable, testable, and explainable.

---

## 2. Decision Flow

The decision process follows this structure:

```text
Farm Context
     +
Environmental Conditions
     ↓
Input Validation
     ↓
Rule Evaluation
     ↓
Risk Score
     ↓
Risk Level
     ↓
Confidence
     ↓
Recommendation
     ↓
Explanation
```

The decision engine is separated from the API and frontend.

This means the same decision logic can be tested independently of HTTP requests or the user interface.

---

## 3. Current MVP Scope

The current decision engine focuses on **water-related risk**.

The primary environmental inputs are:

* Recent rainfall
* Forecast rainfall
* Temperature

The agricultural context includes:

* Crop
* Growth stage

The current workflow is primarily designed around maize.

The architecture allows additional crops, risk types, environmental variables, and agricultural rules to be introduced later.

---

## 4. Environmental Risk Rules

The engine begins with a score of zero.

### Recent Rainfall

If recent rainfall is below 5:

```text
recent_rainfall < 5
    → +2 risk points
```

The engine records:

```text
Low recent rainfall
```

as a contributing factor.

---

### Forecast Rainfall

If forecast rainfall is below 5:

```text
forecast_rainfall < 5
    → +2 risk points
```

The engine records:

```text
Limited forecast rainfall
```

as a contributing factor.

---

### Temperature

If temperature is 35°C or higher:

```text
temperature >= 35
    → +2 risk points
```

The engine records:

```text
High temperature
```

as a contributing factor.

---

## 5. Growth-Stage Rules

The decision engine incorporates crop growth stage because water requirements can vary during the crop lifecycle.

For the current maize-focused workflow:

### Flowering

```text
Flowering
    → +2 risk points
```

Flowering is treated as a particularly water-sensitive stage.

The engine may report:

```text
Maize is in a water-sensitive flowering stage
```

as a contributing factor.

---

### Germination

```text
Germination
    → +1 risk point
```

---

### Vegetative

```text
Vegetative
    → +1 risk point
```

---

### Maturity

```text
Maturity
    → +0 risk points
```

---

## 6. Risk Score

The overall score is calculated by adding the points generated by applicable rules.

Conceptually:

```text
Risk Score =
    Rainfall Risk
  + Forecast Risk
  + Temperature Risk
  + Growth-Stage Adjustment
```

For example:

```text
Low recent rainfall       +2
High temperature          +2
Flowering stage           +2
--------------------------------
Total                     6
```

The resulting score is used to determine the risk level.

---

## 7. Risk Levels

The current MVP uses three risk levels.

### HIGH

```text
Score >= 4
```

A HIGH assessment indicates that multiple risk conditions have combined to produce a significant water-stress concern under the current rule set.

---

### MEDIUM

```text
Score >= 2 and < 4
```

A MEDIUM assessment indicates that one or more conditions have increased water-related risk, but the total score has not reached the HIGH threshold.

---

### LOW

```text
Score < 2
```

A LOW assessment indicates that the current evaluated conditions do not produce a significant water-risk score under the MVP rules.

---

## 8. Confidence

The current confidence representation is deliberately simple.

```text
Score >= 2
    → MEDIUM confidence

Score < 2
    → LOW confidence
```

Confidence should not be interpreted as a statistical probability.

It represents the current MVP's confidence classification based on the amount of rule-based evidence contributing to the assessment.

Future versions may introduce more sophisticated confidence estimation based on:

* Data completeness
* Forecast reliability
* Historical observations
* Sensor quality
* Model performance
* Uncertainty estimation

---

## 9. Recommendations

Recommendations are generated from the resulting risk level and agricultural context.

### HIGH

The current general recommendation is to:

> Monitor soil moisture and consider appropriate water-conservation measures.

When the crop is in a water-sensitive stage, the recommendation can provide additional context.

---

### MEDIUM

The current recommendation is:

> Monitor soil moisture and weather conditions closely.

---

### LOW

The current recommendation is:

> Continue monitoring weather and farm conditions.

---

## 10. Explainability

Explainability is a core design requirement of the FarmOS decision engine.

The engine does not simply return a risk label.

Instead, it identifies the conditions that contributed to the result.

The assessment can therefore be understood as:

```text
Input
  ↓
Applicable Rules
  ↓
Contributing Factors
  ↓
Score
  ↓
Risk Level
  ↓
Recommendation
  ↓
Explanation
```

For example, an assessment could identify:

```text
Low recent rainfall
High temperature
Maize is in a water-sensitive flowering stage
```

The explanation connects these factors to the resulting risk assessment.

This allows farmers, developers, and evaluators to understand why the system produced its recommendation.

---

## 11. Deterministic Design

The current decision engine is deterministic.

Given the same validated inputs, it should produce the same assessment.

For example:

```text
Same Inputs
     ↓
Same Rules
     ↓
Same Score
     ↓
Same Risk Level
```

This provides:

* Reproducibility
* Predictability
* Easier testing
* Easier debugging
* Transparent behavior

Deterministic rules are particularly useful for the MVP because the decision process can be inspected directly rather than requiring interpretation of an opaque model.

---

## 12. Input Validation

The decision engine operates on validated values supplied by the application layer.

Inputs must represent meaningful environmental and agricultural values.

The API layer is responsible for validating request structure and data types before the decision engine is invoked.

The decision engine should not assume that client-provided values are trustworthy simply because they originated from the frontend.

This separation provides:

```text
Untrusted Request
       ↓
API Validation
       ↓
Validated Data
       ↓
Decision Engine
```

---

## 13. Separation from the API

The decision engine is intentionally independent of FastAPI request handling.

The API is responsible for:

* Authentication
* Authorization
* Request validation
* Farm lookup
* Environmental-data retrieval
* Response handling

The decision engine is responsible for:

* Applying agricultural rules
* Calculating the score
* Determining risk
* Identifying factors
* Producing recommendations
* Producing explanations

This separation makes the engine independently testable.

---

## 14. Separation from External Environmental Providers

The decision engine does not need to understand the internal response format of an external weather provider.

The environmental-data layer provides the normalized values required by the engine.

```text
Open-Meteo
    ↓
Environmental Data Layer
    ↓
Normalized Environmental Values
    ↓
Decision Engine
```

This prevents the decision rules from becoming tightly coupled to a particular provider.

A future environmental provider can therefore be introduced without redesigning the decision engine.

---

## 15. Assessment Output

A completed assessment contains the information required to understand and act on the result.

Conceptually:

```text
Assessment
├── Risk Level
├── Score
├── Confidence
├── Factors
├── Recommendation
├── Explanation
└── Farm Context
```

A representative result can contain:

```json
{
  "risk_level": "HIGH",
  "score": 6,
  "confidence": "MEDIUM",
  "factors": [
    "Low recent rainfall",
    "High temperature",
    "Maize is in a water-sensitive flowering stage"
  ],
  "recommendation": "Monitor soil moisture closely and prioritize appropriate water-conservation measures during flowering.",
  "explanation": "Water-stress risk increased because of: Low recent rainfall, High temperature, Maize is in a water-sensitive flowering stage."
}
```

The exact response structure is determined by the API layer.

The important architectural principle is that the decision result contains both the outcome and the reasoning factors behind it.

---

## 16. Persistence

The decision engine produces an assessment that can be persisted by the application layer.

The engine itself does not need to manage database operations.

The separation is:

```text
Decision Engine
      ↓
Assessment Result
      ↓
Application Layer
      ↓
Database
```

This keeps agricultural reasoning separate from storage concerns.

Persisted assessments provide historical information that can later support:

* Farmer review
* Feedback analysis
* System evaluation
* Model improvement
* Agricultural research

---

## 17. Feedback and Future Improvement

FarmOS supports farmer feedback associated with completed assessments.

The current feedback loop is:

```text
Assessment
    ↓
Recommendation
    ↓
Farmer Action
    ↓
Observed Result
    ↓
Feedback
```

Feedback is stored as historical information.

It does **not** automatically change the decision rules or retrain the system.

This is intentional.

Automatic modification of agricultural decision logic based on unvalidated feedback could introduce unpredictable behavior.

Future versions may use accumulated feedback for controlled evaluation and improvement.

---

## 18. Testing Strategy

Because the decision engine is deterministic, its behavior can be tested using known input and expected output combinations.

Tests should cover:

### Low Risk

Normal environmental conditions should produce LOW risk.

### Medium Risk

A condition that crosses the medium threshold should produce MEDIUM risk.

### High Risk

Multiple significant risk conditions should produce HIGH risk.

### Growth Stage

Different growth stages should apply their documented score adjustments.

### Explainability

When a rule is triggered, the corresponding contributing factor should be included in the assessment.

### Boundary Conditions

Important thresholds should be tested explicitly, including:

```text
Rainfall = 5
Temperature = 35
Risk score = 2
Risk score = 4
```

Boundary testing helps ensure that the implemented rules match the documented decision model.

---

## 19. Current Limitations

The current decision engine is intentionally an MVP.

Current limitations include:

* The primary implemented risk model is water-related risk.
* The environmental rules use a small number of variables.
* Thresholds are deterministic and relatively simple.
* The model does not yet incorporate historical climate patterns.
* The model does not yet incorporate soil measurements.
* The model does not yet use satellite or IoT sensor data.
* Confidence is a simple classification rather than a statistical probability.
* Farmer feedback does not automatically modify the rules.
* The current model should not be interpreted as a replacement for professional agricultural advice.

These limitations define the current scope and provide a clear path for future development.

## 20. Future Extensions

The decision-engine architecture can be extended without replacing the existing MVP foundation.

Potential future capabilities include:

* Additional crops
* Additional growth stages
* Soil-moisture data
* Soil type
* Historical rainfall
* Seasonal climate information
* Drought indicators
* Pest and disease risk
* Extreme-weather risk
* Satellite observations
* IoT sensor measurements
* Improved uncertainty estimation
* Machine-learning models

Future AI or machine-learning components should preserve the FarmOS principles of:

* Explainability
* Testability
* Traceability
* Safety
* Human oversight

The deterministic MVP therefore provides a foundation rather than a dead-end implementation.

---

## 21. Design Principles

The FarmOS Decision Engine follows six primary principles:

1. **Correctness** — calculations should follow explicitly defined rules.
2. **Explainability** — assessments should identify contributing factors.
3. **Simplicity** — the MVP should remain understandable and maintainable.
4. **Testability** — deterministic behavior should be independently testable.
5. **Security** — the engine should operate only on validated application inputs.
6. **Extensibility** — new agricultural models and data sources should be possible without rewriting the entire system.

These principles guide both the current implementation and future development of FarmOS.

---

## 22. Summary

The FarmOS Decision Engine converts environmental conditions and farm context into an explainable water-risk assessment.

Its current flow is:

```text
Farm Context
      +
Environmental Conditions
      ↓
Rule Evaluation
      ↓
Risk Score
      ↓
Risk Level
      ↓
Confidence
      ↓
Recommendation
      ↓
Explanation
      ↓
Persistent Assessment
      ↓
Farmer Feedback
```

The engine is deliberately deterministic, transparent, and independently testable.

This provides FarmOS with a clear reasoning foundation that can later be expanded with additional agricultural data, models, and decision-support capabilities without sacrificing explainability.
